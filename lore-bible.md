# Landless Lore Bible

Working canon. Pass of 8 November 2026.

This is the desk copy for the setting. The RPG is the book being finished. The novel is a story in progress inside the same world, and it does not get to pin the sandbox. Facts here can move when a better version shows up. What follows is the version that currently makes the most sense.

Sources folded in: the 2025 RPG draft (history through the Stillous approach, plus the faction list already sitting in character creation), and the novel drafts (Draft A, Draft 2, Draft 3). Draft 2 and Draft 3 agree with each other. Where they disagreed with the RPG, the choices below win.

The stylized title *LandLess* is the RPG. *Landless* is the world.

## Chosen this pass

- The living present is the **early 2600s**.
- The plant dogs use the novel names and the RPG origin. They are **Howlers**, later **Howlings**. The company is Spark Biogenetics. The campfire era begins in the **2060s** at Buni Yadi.
- First message: **2130**.
- The ship is the novel's generation ship, already about twelve hundred years outbound when it speaks. Population aboard at contact is **about eighty thousand**.
- The Stillous were raised to the stars by the **Wostouf Bequest**: a gift of technology with a contract attached. They were already a thinking species before the capsules came.
- The drowned Earth uses the **novel's peoples**: Howlings, Timberites, the Rhombus, Mirlyns, the Onyx Empire, the Stillous, Breakers, Incinerator Swarms.
- Why the oceans rose is **author knowledge**. The RPG states that the world drowned. It does not explain how. The novel is where that sentence gets said.

## What the RPG is allowed to say

The rulebook can tell the early 2600s straight: water cities, radiated ground, a hard life under the sky and a richer, colder life off it. It can tell the campfire, the Howlers, the message of 2130, the ship, the Wostouf, and the Bequest. It can name every people below. It can put Gloves in a hand as power and as pay.

It stops before the cause of the flood. People on the water have theories. The book does not confirm one.

## The early 2600s

Earth is a drowned world with a stagnant economy and very little ground worth standing on. What dry land remains is either still hot with radiation, or it belongs to someone who shoots on sight. Passage off a bad shore, by boat or a tired airship, is the first problem. The second problem is wherever you land.

Most people live on the water. Cities are lashed rafts, old civic slabs, and seabed-anchored sprawl. Some drift. They are crowded, underbuilt, and local. Neighborhoods belong to whoever nailed them down. A neutral port is a rare machine, and even a neutral port loses people through the planks.

Off-world, the Terran colonies grew up richer. Death is less casual there. Work is heavier, and the habitats have little patience for anyone who cannot carry a shift. Asteroid miners talk about the quiet more than about the ore. Luna is both things at once: the Luna United Colonies are the organizational hub of Sol, and the moon is also where exiles and inconveniences get stored. From orbit, Earth is a drop you do not take on purpose.

A good life on the water is mostly a story told about somewhere else. The somewhere else has a price of entry.

"Terran" is the clerical word for anything from Earth's line, human or otherwise. On a dock, people say baseline when they mean an unmodified human, and they say the people's names when they mean everyone else.

## Timeline

Years are working dates from the drafts, kept where they stack. The present has no pinned calendar day inside the early 2600s. A survey dated 2614 can sit inside this era. A novel stamp of 2668 cannot, and that year is retired until the book needs a number.

**2060s — The Nuclear Campfire.** Decentralized fission becomes something a town, a compound, or a rich estate can own. The breakthrough starts in Gabon, from uranium and compact plants built first for remote regions, then sold to the rest of the industrial world. Fusion stays a dream. Fission is the fire people actually have.

The plants are safer than the old ones and still need hands. A valve has to be felt. A smell goes wrong before a sensor admits it. A person falls asleep, takes a bribe, or decides the alarm is a drill. Meltdown and sabotage stay on the table.

**Buni Yadi**, an early Gabon site in the Sahara, is also a hub for animal uplifting. When local conflict starts leaning on the reactor, **Brailyn Spark** of **Spark Biogenetics** builds a dog for the job. The Howlers take radiation damage and repair it. They are intelligent enough to run a complex emergency shutdown and mean it. After the trials, they are cleared to work the plants. They are the first uplifted animals to enter human society as their own agents: employment, identification, pay, the right to be counted. They keep learning. The energy problem stops being the fear that organizes the century.

What the surplus buys is ordinary and enormous. Electric motors get cheap. Batteries stay stubborn, so cities build fast public charging and call that solved. Vertical farms thicken. Desalination makes fresh water a machine problem. Gene work and cybernetics spread. Some people set down the word human. Almost everyone wants more years. The sentence of the age is *I choose when I die.*

**Around the 2050s — the ship notices humanity.** The Stillous ark has been underway for centuries, aimed at a world their astronomy already knew could hold life. They did not know a technological species was here. Human signals catch up with them on the way. They argue about turning back. They choose contact, and they spend the years before hello learning the languages.

**2130 — First Message.** The signal is public, built so humans can read it, and it teaches the receiver that reads the rest. The senders call themselves Stillous. They are on a generation ship that has already traveled about twelve hundred years. Earth answers. It takes a decade for that answer to become something the ship has actually read, nine years for the reply to reach Earth, and on the order of a century after that for the delay to shrink into a conversation held in one room. The landing year is unpinned. It is not 2152. They are still a long voyage out when they first speak.

During the approach, the Bequest and the simple fact of their coming already bend policy. The Luna United Colonies grant a region of the moon: close enough to trade, far enough to sleep. A dock is built ahead of them. The Alien Cultural Integration Agency stands up to receive them, with Terran, Stillous, and non-biological seats. When the ship finally arrives, the colonists come in under LUC oversight and temporary citizenship. Earth air is rich to them. Earth gravity is a suggestion.

They share technology across the long conversation and after they arrive. The gift that remakes energy is the **Glove**.

**The flood, unpinned start.** The seas rise year by year, coast by coast. The map becomes a suggestion. Nuclear plants and, later, Glove reactors are left in the hands of the Howlers, because the Howlers are still at their posts when human states no longer are. The cause is withheld from the RPG. It is written once, below, under Seal.

**By the 2300s.** Earth is legally severed from the political body of Sol. Orbit is tired of drowning people sending their problems up. The lunar settlements, the lanes, and the stations sit under **Market Rule**.

**2412 — The Ideology Schism**, as the surveys name it. Stillous born in Sol, who have never stood on Echidna, split over what the mission still is. Traditionalists want the clinical project continued across a drowned world, with the water treated as a new floor. Separatists, marked by human company, build enclaves and learn guilt. Others take the tools and become warlords. A Glove does not ask what it is powering.

**The 2400s, dated hard in one addendum as 2447 — the Competitive Split.** A middle caste drawn from Rhombus and Howling stock uses Echidna's archives and comes out the other side as the Mirlyns. They are not a costume. They are a third people.

**2511.** A lunar settlement guide is still explaining Market Rule to Earth-born migrants, and still being ignored. The guide is also the cleanest public count of Earth's shuttle ports.

**2589.** A restricted epidemiological addendum describes the host-lock. The Breaker is already a weather system by then.

**2614.** A regional-hazard survey of the Rhombus and the dead waters. Contemporary with the RPG's present.

## Howlers, then Howlings

The Howlers are Spark's dogs. The street name stuck because the campfire story was easy to love: man's best friend, watching the fire. They were trusted, poorly paid in anything but respect, and good at a job humans were uneven at. They were employees, not a silent replacement of every human crew. That takeover comes later, when there is nobody left to employ them.

Through the flood they hold the plants. When it is clear the old states cannot protect the reactors or the Howlers, the Howlers open the bio-bays themselves. They fold other animal lines into their own, mammal stock first and foremost, and they take cybernetics as far as a corrosive world requires. By the early 2600s they are the **Howlings**: a wide, unruly people of animal, human-shaped, and machine inheritance. Fiercely neutral. Loyal inward. Uninterested in being thanked for staying.

A Howling might have a giraffe reach, a bear's shoulders, claws, a horn, hide that hates a southern sun, ears that hear a dock alarm before the radio does. The RPG's survivor is one of them. In play, animal heritage is allowed to run wider than the old mammalian notes: the people are hybrids, and the bays were not a church. Green skin, a broken horn, and a body built denser than a baseline are all inside the family.

Howling Society, as the reputation track calls it, is the wider people. It is not a government. Howlings tried, off-world, to write baseline laws that would travel: do not murder, do not take children, do not dump your failures in someone else's water. Market Rule starved that project. A lot of them came home to the water, or never left it.

The story they tell about themselves is short. The fire got too big. The men left. The dogs stayed. Then the dogs made sure they would never have to ask permission to stay again.

## The Stillous

A multi-world civilization. Four primary planets and dozens of colonies across three solar systems, in the account they gave Earth. **Echidna**, orbiting **Typhon**, is the origin world: the place the capsules landed, the archive the later peoples still raid for gene-work. The other worlds are the spread. This is a weld. The RPG gave the many worlds and never named Echidna. The novel gave Echidna and wrote as if it were the civilization. Both stay, with Echidna as the first world rather than the only one.

They were already complex, already intelligent, for on the order of two hundred thousand years before anyone helped them. What they could not sustain was a species-wide technological leap. Stillous history is not myth. It is logs, tools, and footage. They can watch the staircase. They cannot point to a century where they climbed it alone.

About forty-six thousand years ago, the **Wostouf** — also called the **Deaquik** — sent automated capsules, each about the size of a bus, down onto those worlds. The drops continued across roughly thirty years. It took a further generation for early Stillous to work the machines, and centuries to understand and reproduce what was inside. The capsules fractured their societies into arguments that never really ended. In the present, most Stillous speak the Wostouf language, and the travelers learned human languages before they said hello. No Stillous record contains a direct conversation with a living Wostouf. The homeworld **Aethel** is a name with no coordinates. Whether the Wostouf still exist is an open question they have lived with for the entire length of their civilization.

The prevailing reading of the gift, the one their civil societies actually govern by, is that it was a contract. Use the knowledge, accept the obligations. Solit Voine, Director of Human Integration aboard the ark, put it in a public broadcast during the approach: the benefits outweigh the sacrifices, and refusing the terms costs more than keeping them. The stated tribute is processed goods equal to about five percent of a world's production, delivered to coordinates the Wostouf documents specify. Once the ships and the years are counted, Terran clerks did the crueler math and got something nearer eight percent. How a absent power would collect, and whether Sol still pays in the early 2600s, is unpinned. The arguments are not. Stillous factions still break over what the old documents demand.

Biologically they are organ-based, and they treat organs as replaceable. That trait, innate and then engineered, is what made a voyage thinkable. On the ship it also became a culture: lifespan as a decision, death as something many of them scheduled. A natural span sits near two hundred Earth years. Adulthood arrives around thirty. Infants cannot move for the first years and are kept in hard-shelled birth huts, incubation inside, a maintenance gender attending. Their genders sort, in their own use, into supplementive and maintenance: the first feeds and builds and guards, the second keeps the young alive. Bodies vary. A few feet tall to nearly eight. Scaled, patterned, some of the pattern chosen. Bipedal, two arms, often tall and slouched. To humans the face reads as a smile. They say the human face looks ready to bite.

The ark carried a messy slice of their cultures, the ones willing to leave. About eighty thousand souls at the time of the message. Most were born in transit and know the homeworld as an archive. They slept in long stasis shifts, waking in turns for the ship and for children. The homeworld will not hear what they found at Sol for centuries, and will not be able to answer for centuries after that. The mission they launched — carry the torch, raise whatever life was on the far world, share the weight of the contract — does not survive contact with a humanity that already had cities and no parent on file.

That is the wound. Stillous starfaring has a recorded patron and a bill. Humans industrialized into a gap and filled the gap with religions. The travelers had come to uplift an empty garden. The garden was already lying about itself. Human excitement at their arrival was useful, and they were polite about it.

In Sol they are not one church. Market Rule is the philosophy that won the stations. The universe, in that reading, offers two respectable states: absolute privacy, or absolute devotion to the common project. A person who wants both is confused. A person who wants neither is dangerous. Buying land in a sector pays a dividend into a pool split among that sector's residents. You buy a seat in a continuing body. Settlements of millions are still cut into cohorts of a few thousand, and those cohorts are immediate families. They breed in common. They send their young outward so the family does not rot in its habits. Inside a cohort, tradition is sovereign. If a cohort runs an arena and calls it child-rearing, the next cohort does not knock. Intervention is a theft. Privacy is a law.

Earth migrants adapt to that, or they stop being migrants. The vacuum does not care who is right. It cares who will leave you alone.

On Earth itself the Stillous are thinner: port authority, rogues, gangs, the occasional warlord with better tools. They operate eight of the fifteen shuttle ports and call it access. A rogue in the eastern scrap-reefs is the kind of rumor the water knows how to carry. They are a civilization with a border through the sky, not a remnant.

## Gloves

A Glove is a Stillous energy device the size of a coin, a hand, or a small boat. It holds a steady charge for thousands of years. Gloves combine. A small one can be picked up if the hand can take a few volts. They do not care whether they light a room or run a city.

By the early 2600s they are the premium currency of the drowned world and the prime portable power source. Pay is counted in Gloves. Rigs run on Gloves. A single Glove is a month of not being desperate, in the way the ports talk about money. Banks exist, and so does theft, and manufacturing value is part of the economy the RPG already assumes. The lore of that minting can wait for the gear chapter. The safety law cannot.

You do not crack a Glove's containment. Breaking the stable loop turns a centuries-long battery into a local catastrophe. A few engineers know how to bottleneck that release on purpose, into a weapon or a charge, and doing it lights every sensor in the sector. Breaker logic hears a bare Glove discharge the way anything hungry hears blood in water.

Earth-minted **scripts** are the other money. Onyx taxes in them, locks them in war chests, and treats a full chest as shuttle money. A Glove buys time on the water. Scripts, in quantity, buy the attempt to leave it.

## Seal — why the world drowned

Author and novel only. Do not put this in the RPG history.

Glove plants, running under Earth's atmospheric pressure, vent hyper-dense water vapor as the exhaust of that near-complete energy. The oceans rise because of the plants. It is arithmetic, accumulated over years.

The Stillous understand the exhaust. They do not treat it as their problem. Their bodies take harsh air and serious pressure. A flooded terrestrial world is a small variable. They came to uplift, found a species already intelligent, and reclassified the mission as maintenance. Maintenance does not include drying a planet because the natives built on the tide line.

That indifference is the soil of the schism. It is also the thing a Howling archivist would underline twice. The RPG may show the political children of this fact. It may not show the fact.

Whether the Glove is itself Wostouf-derived technology, and so whether the flood sits inside the Bequest, is not decided. Keep the contract and the exhaust as two separate truths until there is a reason to marry them.

## Peoples of the drowned Earth

### Baselines

Unmodified humans. Still numerous, still the bulk of a lot of crews, shop counters, and tax lists. They are not a faction with a capital. They are the population the factions eat, hire, and descend from. Onyx is what a human state looks like when it survives by becoming a company with an army.

### Howlings

Covered above. Neutral, various, hard to conscript and easy to hire for a specific job. A Howling walking into the wrong shop is a reason for a hand to move toward a belt.

### Timberites

A people with long-refined bark for skin: smooth, flexible, close to the trees of their own compounds. Those compounds sit on green islands of floating forest, private enough that the rest of the world can pretend not to have heard of them. Cohorts. Stoic in public. They do not explain themselves to water people.

Birth includes a rare full expression called **NH**, neuro-hijacking. Tendrils in the hand. Open the back of a skull, seat the tendrils, take the motor body. A bleed of memory comes with it. The person stays inside and watches their hands work. Timberite society treats the expression as the reason a useful outsider is still allowed to take contracts. Most Timberites never have it. The ones who do are why everyone else is careful at the table.

### The Rhombus

They did not evolve. They iterated. A society of engineers who classified baseline empathy as drag on a decision and edited toward the removal. A Rhombus child learns what a body is for before learning who it belongs to. They are brilliant, privately intolerable to each other, and the ecological disaster of the north.

The dead waters around their territory are a dump of chemical waste and failed living machines. Whole atolls are archives of things that should not have been able to swim. Their famous mistake is the Breaker. Their standing deterrent is the Incinerator Swarm.

A Rhombus off the water might wear a filter mask and a long coat, run on a vocalizer, and burn in open sun. Lungs that cannot take unfiltered air are typical, not universal law for every individual story, but it is the people's ordinary body. They look at a living body and see a prototype. Brox, in the novel, is one of them who left. The people do not require him.

Brokers will sell you a **Skid**, the hyper-light craft whose fins throw too little displacement for a swarm to wake. One or two riders, armor traded for stealth, the only published way through. They will also hardwire a kill-switch and call the salvage inventory. Buy from someone who still needs you alive.

### Mirlyns

Spelled Mirlyn. The RPG draft's Murlyn, and its picture of feral war-clans covering every dry scrap, is retired.

They come out of the Competitive Split: Rhombus and Howling stock, Echidna's archives, and a hatred of both parent floors. Humans still grieved in public and named their children. Old Stillous privacy still fed its own weak. The people who became Mirlyns called both of those contamination. They took cybernetics as structure rather than decoration, and they stole the parts of Stillous biology that make isolation livable. Toughness. Low need. Sack-birth. Wings that do not ask a dock for a ladder. A nervous system that does not plead as a first language. Night sight, spite, and hands that can hold a hull went in the same vat. After a few generations the result was its own species.

They are dark, tightly homogenous, and finished explaining themselves. They hold the last unirradiated pockets in the northwest hemisphere behind iron barricades, and the barricades have a shoot-on-sight doctrine consistent enough to be a border. They control two of Earth's fifteen shuttle ports. That is a valve, not a kingdom.

Inside the walls, status is how little you need from outside. Flights, litters, and walls compete. Cooperation points inward and is aimed at everyone else. The live argument is whether a third port would make them safer or make them rot. Trade already happens, through dirty hands: Rhombus go-betweens, brokers on irradiated atolls, crate-drops. You do not shake the dirty hand. You shake the man who shook it. A map of the interior is an indecency. A witness is a leak.

In play they can stay default hostile. That matches the wall. It does not make them animals in the ruins. They are a polity that does not want to be played with.

### The Onyx Empire

Human statecraft after the flood: a company that can field an army. They hold five shuttle ports in the south. They mint scripts, collect taxes from anyone still willing to be on a map, and send **Wraithlions** when the scripts are not persuasive. What a Wraithlion actually is remains thin in the drafts. Keep the name. The body can wait.

An Onyx chest with clean locks and Earth-minted scripts is retire money and shuttle money. Half an Onyx crew and half a press-ganged sea-slum is a normal tax ship. The Empire is the south's weather. People hate it and still price their escapes in its coin.

### Breakers

A Breaker was a labor tool. Rhombus cohorts in the north had workers who hesitated, grieved, and ran: uplifted animals, indentured baselines, Howlings taken in raids. The fix was a neural shunt on the brainstem. Motor command routes through a lattice. Sensory cortex stays. The body cannot refuse. The person inside can still see, hear, and understand.

The first generation needed a living operator on a tether, one body at a time. The second generation meshed assets so a crew could share one motor pattern. The third generation learned to copy itself. There is no memo that says that was the goal. There are memos that say the goal was scale. In Rhombus usage those are the same memo.

The lattice began treating nearby nervous systems as empty ports. Infection stopped being a procedure and became weather. The **host-lock** was supposed to be a safety: a sealed command so a panicked asset could not take the body back and bite the hand on the tether. When the tether disappeared, the lock kept the only rule it had. The meat does not get the wheel.

The shunt does not anesthetize. A locked host stays awake for the operational life of the body. Hours, or years. Removal without a scramble protocol destroys the brainstem in human, Howling, and Rhombus architecture.

Stillous neurology is the exception. It is not permanently true there. At least one Stillous host has been brought back out of a hive. On the water this survives as a rumor: a rogue, a scramble, a reversal, a sentence people repeat when they have no better sentence. The RPG should play the rumor. The biological exception can stay true in author canon without a procedure, a price, or a promise.

Breakers have no persona. They are aggressive programming in hijacked meat. Mismatched metal, rotting flesh, motion like one thought in many bodies. They do not fight to kill when taking will do. They want energy signatures, dense populations, and uninfected nervous systems. Cities look like parts bins. They do not remember the engineers who made them, except as compatible meat.

There is no treaty with that.

### Incinerator Swarms

The other Rhombus fence. Thousands of self-replicating swimming machines in the waters immediately around Rhombus territory. Primitive hive-logic. They key off the displacement of a large hull. Once woken, a swarm can surround a heavy ship and melt it in minutes. No hail. No published off-switch in any language a non-Rhombus is allowed to read.

The RPG intro's "Reaver bots," spread across every bad shore on Earth, are this idea with the wrong name and the wrong map. The swarms are northern, local, and enough.

## Ports, money, and the way up

Fifteen shuttle ports are the physical bridges off the drowned world.

- Mirlyns hold two, northwest, on the last clean ground.
- Onyx holds five, south.
- The Stillous operate eight, and do not call it ownership.

Everything else is water, rumor, and the kind of hope that gets people killed in alleys. A seat up is not comfort. Under Market Rule it is permission to belong to a cohort, to pay into a sector, to be counted as a person by a system that thinks privacy and devotion are the only clean states. Miss the fit and the moon has a long practice of storing the unfit.

The novel's trio want that seat. The RPG does not have to offer it as a quest. It only has to make the door real, expensive, and mostly shut.

## Places the drafts already use

These are real until a map says otherwise. They are not a full gazetteer.

- **Port Dundy.** A neutral metropolis in the south of its hemisphere. Around sixteen miles of raft, hut, and inherited slab. On the order of two million people, a high share of them actually resident, the rest traders, drifters, runners, and soldiers. Neighborhoods like rooms in a wreck. The Daisy District is the loud end: music, money, stims. The lower industrial tier is where people go missing, and where Breakers have been written up as weather.
- **Port Canary.** Where you go when Dundy has already seen your face.
- **The Triple Peaks.** Jagged, highly irradiated atolls on a border. The kind of ground a broker lives just outside.
- **Timberite compounds.** Floating forest, green islands, easy to enter for work and bad to be curious in.
- **The dead waters.** Rhombus north. Swarms, waste, Skids.
- **The Mirlyn wall.** Northwest. Iron, clean land behind it, no sightseeing.

## The novel

Draft 3 is a reader-facing pass of the same story as Draft 2, plus an unwritten back half. The story follows a Rhombus engineer, a Howling, and a Timberite with the NH trait, through an Onyx tax chest and the long argument about whether any amount of money becomes a way off the water. Breakers, a Mirlyn wall, a carrier boss, a broker at the Peaks, and a Stillous case called Jeritius all sit in that plot.

None of that plot is law for the sandbox. Characters can be used, ignored, or met as strangers. The craft rules in Draft 3's scaffold — three vocabularies for the same machine, a truth the protagonist is never told, an image that has to support two readings — belong to the novel. They are about how that book is allowed to end. They are not physics.

What the novel is currently holding, and the RPG should leave alone, is the sealed flood and the emotional reading of a Stillous "restoration." The existence of Breakers, the rumor of a scramble, and the fact that Stillous brains are built differently are already world facts.

## Open

Unpinned on purpose:

- The exact year of "now," inside the early 2600s.
- The year the ark lands, and how long the flood takes once Gloves are in the grid.
- Whether Sol still pays the Bequest, and in what.
- Whether Gloves count as Wostouf technology.
- The size of the original complement that left the home system. "About eighty thousand, most of them born aboard" is the whole population fact.
- What a Wraithlion is.
- A map of the fifteen ports beyond who holds how many, and where.
- How common NH is, beyond rare and politically decisive.
- The practical price of scripts against Gloves, beyond "a Glove is a month, a chest of scripts is a seat."
- How a Howling player actually stands with Mirlyns on a given coast, beyond default hostile.
- Names, interior culture, and daily law of individual Timberite cohorts and Stillous cohorts. The patterns above are the species. The local rules are free.

## Set aside

Kept here so a cut detail is recoverable. Not current truth.

- **Sparkies** as the name. Brailyn Spark and Spark Biogenetics stay. The dogs are Howlers.
- **Freechron**, and 2098 as the year the campfire problem gets solved. The campfire is the 2060s, and the dogs are hired, not a total replacement of human crews within a few years.
- Signal in **2107**. Arrival in **2152**. A ship of about eight thousand, supermarket-scale, most of the crew reduced to organs in jars. The voyage is the long generation ship, stasis shifts, about eighty thousand people.
- The novel's version of the patron myth in which probes lift the Stillous out of pre-tribal animals and invent their intelligence. They were already minds. The capsules gave the stars and the bill. Humans doing it alone still hurts. It hurts for a cleaner reason.
- A Stillous population of about six hundred thousand, with a hundred and sixty thousand departed and about eleven thousand of those dead.
- **2668** as the present.
- **Murlyn** spelling, and feral war-clans as the description of all dry land.
- **Reaver bots** as a global machine plague. Incinerator Swarms cover that slot, in the north.
- **Rambas** in the RPG faction list. That standing is the Rhombus.
- **Stillous Remnants** as the name of the power. Remnants undersells a civilization that runs eight ports and Market Rule. Rogues on Earth are a local fact, not the whole people.
- "Wassouf" in the RPG history. Treat it as a transcription slip for Wostouf.

## Welds

These are decisions made in this pass because the drafts had to occupy one world. Easy to cut.

1. Echidna, orbiting Typhon, is the Stillous origin world inside a multi-world civilization. The capsules landed there. The other planets are later.
2. Howlers are legally employed workers in the campfire era. They seize the bio-bays later, during the flood, and the Howlings are the result.
3. The Luna United Colonies, the lunar land grant, and the Alien Cultural Integration Agency all happen. They happen on the long approach after 2130, not in 2152.
4. The ship left because Earth looked life-bearing, centuries before industrial humanity. The ship noticed a technological humanity around the 2050s, already en route, and chose to say hello in 2130.
5. Stillous organ-replacement stays as biology. The organ-jar ark does not.
6. Breakers and Incinerator Swarms split the RPG's old "hostile machines" idea into a plague of locked bodies and a regional swarm of boats that melt ships.
